<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#383838ff;"><b><i>El Time Blocking es una técnica de productividad que consiste en planificar el día por adelantado y reservar determinados bloques de tiempo para hacer tareas específicas.</i></b></span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#383838ff;">Porque</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#383838ff;"><b>las listas de tareas infinitas no funcionan</b></span><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#383838ff;">. Por el simple hecho de que siempre hay más cosas por hacer y te incitan a trabajar más y más.</span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#383838ff;">Así que no solo es importante saber QUÉ tareas tienes que hacer, sino CUÁNDO vas a hacerlas.</span>

<span style="font-size:12.342857360839844pt;">Un timebox (cajas de tiempo) es un periodo de tiempo definido durante el cual el equipo/la persona debe realizar un tarea. Se trata de periodos cortos de tiempo, de entre 5 y 120 minutos y se utilizan para administrar el tiempo que, por ejemplo, un equipo de diseño y desarrollo de producto dispone para terminar un requerimiento dentro de un sprint.</span> 
<span style="font-size:12.342857360839844pt;">En lugar de permitir que el trabajo del equipo continúe hasta que se alcance el objetivo y evaluar el tiempo utilizado,</span> <span style="font-size:12.342857360839844pt;color:#fb5f2cff;">el enfoque timebox consiste en detener el trabajo cuando se alcanza el límite de tiempo y evaluar lo que se hizo.</span> 

<span style="font-size:10.285714149475098pt;">En el Timeboxing se decide  justo antes de empezar una tarea cuánto tiempo quieres dedicar a la ejecución.</span> <span style="font-family:Verdana;font-size:10.285714149475098pt;">La técnica del timebox consiste en fijar el tiempo máximo para conseguir unos objetivos, tomar una decisión o realizar unas tareas, y hacer lo mejor que podamos en ese intervalo. De esta manera, en lugar de ponerse a trabajar en algo hasta que esté hecho, de antemano se acuerda sólo se dedica un tiempo limitado.  La consciencia de esta limitación temporal favorece la priorización de objetivos/tareas y fuerza la toma de decisiones.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>Time blocking</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">and</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>timeboxing</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">both involve allocating fixed</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>time</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">periods to activities - but, while</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>time blocking</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">includes strictly reserving</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>time</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">for an activity,</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>timeboxing</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">includes limiting the</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;"><b>time</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#202124ff;">you spend on it.</span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Time blocking se parece mucho a time boxing, task batching, and day theming.</span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Try time blocking if you...</span>
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Juggle many different projects/responsibilities (Jack Dorsey uses day theming to run two major companies at the same time)</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Spend too much time in “reactive mode”, responding to email and messages</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Find their day chopped up by meetings</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Battle constant interruptions throughout the day</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">Struggle to find the time and mental space for big-picture thinking</span> 


<span style="font-size:15.428571701049805pt;"><b>What is time blocking?</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">{{NotesAttachment:7699ce6ce9e7befaf2e6c35421dfbc0a}}</span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Time blocking is a time management method that asks you to divide your day into blocks of time. Each block is dedicated to accomplishing a specific task, or group of tasks, and only those specific tasks. Instead of keeping an open-ended to-do list of things you’ll get to as you’re able, you’ll start each day with a concrete</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>schedule that lays out what you’ll work on and when</b></span><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">The key to this method is prioritizing your task list in advance – a dedicated weekly review is a must. Take stock of what’s coming up for the week ahead and make a rough sketch of your time blocks for each day. At the</span> <a href="https://doist.com/blog/end-work-day/?itm_campaign=time_blocking&itm_medium=referral&itm_source=productivity_methods_guides" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>end of every workday</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">, review any tasks you didn’t finish – as well as any new tasks that have come in – and adjust your time blocks for the rest of the week accordingly.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#1f1f1fff;">With days that are time blocked in advance, you won’t have to constantly make choices about what to focus on. All you need to do is follow your time blocked schedule. If you get off-task or distracted, simply look at your schedule and get back to whichever task you blocked off time for.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;"><b>Here are three tips to help you with time blocking:</b></span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#08080ff;"><b>Don’t be precise</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">What this means is</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;"><b>block time</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">out for</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;"><b>the type of work you are doing not the specific task</b></span><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">. For example; rather than “complete ABC report”, block time out for “report writing” or “writing time”.</span>  
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">The reason for this is you maintain some degree of flexibility to work on whatever needs doing that is associated with writing.</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">Priorities change rapidly during the week and deadlines change. You want to know you have time to deal with the priorities of the day.</span>  

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#08080ff;"><b>Use your task manager for the specifics</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">What this means look at your calendar and see you have a block of time for writing a proposal. You can then go to your task-manager and group together all tasks associated with writing proposals.</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">Your task-manager is fluid. It will change daily and here you can easily designate your priorities for the day.</span>  

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#08080ff;"><b>Create your weekly blocks as recurring blocks</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">A trick I learned recently was to create recurring blocks of time for the things you want to do each week and set them to recurring at 9 AM Monday. This way, when you do your weekly planning session you can move them around your calendar to fit in with your schedule for the week.</span>  
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">This means if you want time to exercise each week, create however many blocks you want for exercise each week and move them into place when you do your weekly planning. Same for family time, me-time and anything else you want time for each week.</span>  
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">This will keep you focused on what’s important each week.</span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;color:#666565ff;">Time blocking is a great way to make sure you are doing the things that you want time for. The key is not to overcomplicate it and to make sure you keep some free time each week for dealing with emergencies.</span>
