<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Las sesiones de trabajo en eventos son herramientas valiosas a disposición del organizador de la conferencia, siempre y cuando estén enfocadas y tengan un propósito. Sin un objetivo claro, en el mejor de los casos son una pérdida de tiempo y, en el peor, una fuente de frustración, tanto para los participantes como para usted.</span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Esta guía sugiere estrategias y da consejos para diseñar y facilitar sesiones de trabajo que los asistentes aprecien y disfruten.</span> 

<span style="font-size:15.428571701049805pt;"><b>¿Qué es una sesión de trabajo en paralelo?</b></span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Una sesión de trabajo o reunión de trabajo en paralelo es una actividad de grupo tipo taller diseñada para la resolución de problemas, generalmente (pero no siempre) centrada en el tema de la conferencia o del evento. Estas sesiones son más breves que otros puntos del programa del evento. A menudo, los participantes en las sesiones de trabajo se reúnen con todo el grupo para presentar o debatir los resultados del taller.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Event breakout sessions provide opportunities to:</b></span>
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Learn, polish, or share skills</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Discuss common issues</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Brainstorm solutions</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Explore ideas in a less structured format</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Break up the</span> <a href="https://www.socialtables.com/blog/event-planning/event-agenda-design/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>overall event schedule</u></a> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Regain attendees’ attention</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>The benefits of event breakout workshops:</b></span>  
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Improved attendee engagement</span>  
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Time and space for deeper dives into relevant topics</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Targeted content that’s of interest to some, but not all attendees</span>  
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Targeted content across experience and skill levels (i.e., beginner and advanced)</span>   
<span style="font-size:15.428571701049805pt;"><b>What makes a good event breakout session?</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Useful and enjoyable event breakout sessions are often related to the event theme. This way, the subject matter is relevant to attendees. The best event breakout sessions include a participatory element that builds engagement and distinguishes the breakout from more passive conference moments, such as the keynote and panel discussions. They also have facilitators who know how to lead sessions and draw participants out, without being overbearing.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">No one breakout session arrangement stands out as better than the rest. The beauty of the breakout is your ability to tailor it to the needs of your event and audience. There are workshop opportunities for groups of all sizes, whether you’re hosting a large convention or smaller seminar. Follow these tips to design the best breakout options for your event:</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>1. Get input from attendees with pre-event surveys</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Poll attendees ahead of time to see what experiences they’re looking for, then design the event’s breakout options to fit that wishlist. After you gauge interest, find speakers, leaders, and facilitators to meet those needs. Use the</span> <a href="https://www.socialtables.com/blog/event-technology/big-data/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>attendee data you collect</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">to create your breakout programs. Sessions can be track-specific, theme-specific, or unrelated to any specific theme. What matters is that your attendees feel the sessions add value to the event.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>2. Pick the right breakout format</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">First, determine what an effective session means in terms of the specific event. Is your goal to increase engagement? An interactive breakout workshop may be the best format. Sharing case studies, trends, and statistics may be better suited for a presentation-style session. If the aim is to provide time to unwind, look for participatory sessions that get attendees moving, relaxing, or creating.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>3. Ask guests to register for breakout sessions early</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Pre-registration ensures you’ve got adequate materials, space, and staff necessary to host the workshops. It can even reveal less-popular options that may need to be adjusted, promoted, or cut from the schedule.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Even with a pre-registration requirement, people may be looking for an opportunity to join a session on the day of the event.  Have a plan for unregistered attendees—prepare for an influx of last-minute attendees with extra seats and materials, and consider day-of registration to fill empty seats. Keep in mind:</span>

- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>There are drawbacks to breakout session pre-registration.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Attendees may change their mind about sessions they’ve registered for, leaving empty seats and a smaller audience. Allow people to sign up on a standby list so empty seats aren’t left that way.</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Drop-in activities have value, too.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Including drop-in sessions—a morning networking cafe, mid-day mindfulness exercise, or afternoon free-write activity—can break up an agenda, giving guests an energy boost for better focus during subsequent sessions. A mixture of registration-required and open-door options can provide the balance attendees seek.</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>4. Time your breakouts strategically</b></span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Timing is about more than</span> <a href="https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/business-meetings-411-building-better-breakouts" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>staying on schedule</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">, though that’s important as well. If your breakout sessions are scheduled too late in the day, attendees may be more focused on going home and engagement may suffer. Scheduling breakout groups too early may earn fewer registrants.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Don’t overdo it, either: Too many sessions can leave you with burnt-out attendees. Consider whether networking opportunities or an additional break is the better option, and recognize when a session isn’t ideal for a breakout-style format.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>5. Design seating to complement the breakout format</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Certain seating arrangements leave specific impressions. Use</span> <a href="https://www.socialtables.com/product/seat/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>floor plan software to design event seating layouts</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">for each session. Tailor each room layout to the session style.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Collaborative seating</b></span><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">—square or U-shaped table arrangements, semi-circle seating, cafe tables, or hybrid layouts foster communication and action. Theater-style and lecture seating are better for less interactive sessions.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>For on-the-move sessions</b></span><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">, skip tables altogether. Instead, include unexpected seating options like bean bag chairs, or incorporate motion and creative thinking with</span> <a href="https://parade.com/913772/ericasweeney/what-is-a-bosu-ball/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>BOSU Balls</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">instead of chairs.</span> 

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>There’s more to seating arrangements than</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b><i>just</i></b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>the seats.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">You need the right location and meeting space, too. Breakout spaces need to inspire and engage, not overwhelm. When choosing the breakout rooms, consider these factors. Does the room allow:</span>

- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">The appropriate A/V equipment</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Plenty of space to move and interact</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Minimal distractions. (i.e., echoes, clunky heating units, traffic noises, or hallway chatter)</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Environmental comfort (i.e., not too hot, cold, bright, dark, or stuffy)</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>6. Use tech to boost breakout session success</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Encourage participation and make it easier to share ideas with tech-based tools:</span>

- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">A</span> <a href="https://zapier.com/blog/best-poll-apps/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>live polling app</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">like Glisser or sli.do can gather and display audience responses in real-time. Use it to engage with participants, gauge experience levels through polls, determine what they’re most interested in learning, or collect data for future use.</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Test coordination and get attendees moving with a throwable microphone.</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Use a digital whiteboard app to display workshop group notes on a big screen for full-group discussion, and reference during the final presentation.</span> <a href="https://stormboard.com/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Stormboard</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">is one option that can connect to Slack and Trello for easy sharing and reference after the event. Collaboration doesn’t need to stop at the end of the conference: Shared notes can become a living document that attendees revisit after the conference.</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>7. Choose breakout session facilitators who guide without controlling</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Facilitators should provide support relevant to the topic at hand and drive activities, all without disrupting the process. While the session facilitator is in a leadership role, the purpose is to encourage independent problem-solving and idea generation. Rather than telling participants what to think, the facilitator should encourage them</span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><i>to</i></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">think.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Facilitating breakout sessions involves:</b></span>

- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Engaging participants</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Maintaining pace and adjusting as necessary</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Monitoring breakout activities, without injecting expertise into a team discussion</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Overseeing post-workshop discussions and offering answers when asked, rather than</span> <a href="https://medium.com/the-year-of-the-looking-glass/dont-be-a-gatekeeper-5c48db173662" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>acting as a gatekeeper</u></a> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Recording results and leading a closing group discussion</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">The facilitator can’t do it all alone. Provide session moderators for each breakout group. The moderator can introduce the breakout speaker, keep an eye on the clock, hand out necessary materials, and monitor the process.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>8. Build buzz to improve breakout session participation</b></span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Interactive games and activities help improve attendance and engagement during event breakout sessions. Some possibilities to consider:</span>

- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Hand out raffle tickets in exchange for attendance, and offer additional tickets for participation.</span> 
- <a href="https://helloendless.com/how-to-use-event-gamification-and-boost-the-energy-of-the-attendees/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Gamify the experience</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">by sorting participants into teams and awarding points for participation.</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Reward attendees for reaching goals with stickers, branded pens, app download codes, gift cards, or carnival-style prizes.</span> 
- <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create ‘secret,’ invite-only workshops to encourage participation. Allow participants to unlock invitations by visiting a specific number of booths, finishing a scavenger hunt, or attending a pre-set session schedule.</span> 
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>9. Ensure breakout workshops cater to a variety of learning styles</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Learning is an individual process without a one-size-fits-all solution. Still, there are</span> <a href="https://omnipress.com/blog/educational-ideas-that-will-improve-your-conference-sessions/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>three main learning styles</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">to target. Each person often has a prominent style that works best for them, whether they know it or not. Incorporating tools to increase comprehension and focus attention can improve the participants’ experiences, no matter their style.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Kinesthetic learners learn by doing.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Keep kinesthetic learners focused with pipe cleaners, Legos, or Plus-Plus blocks—tactile tools to improve memory and focus. Asking kinesthetic learners to handle note taking can increase participation and help them retain the information, as well. Kinesthetics will likely outnumber visual or auditory individuals in participatory or active sessions—it’s where they shine.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Visual learners thrive with visual aids.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Visual learners are fond of text and learn best by watching demonstrations before trying something on their own. The front row will likely include more visual learners than kinesthetic or auditory learners. Slides, images, graphs, and handouts are perfect for visual learners—just avoid clutter; cramming too much on one page can overwhelm the senses. Physical note taking can improve comprehension and retention for visual learners, so include space on handouts for jotted notes.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Auditory learners are verbal and conversational.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Incorporate questions, dialogue, and discussion to increase participation and improve retention in auditory learners. Auditory learners are often the first to speak up and value discussion. For this learning style, spoken directions and verbal explanations are easier to understand, but too much noise can be a distraction, so limit non-essential background noise to improve concentration.</span>

<span style="font-size:15.428571701049805pt;"><b>Go beyond lectures with these breakout session ideas</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Interactive event breakout sessions</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>1.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Use the</span> <a href="http://kstoolkit.org/Human+Spectrogram" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Human Spectrogram technique</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">to uncover common industry struggles—then collaborate in small groups to find ways to solve these challenges.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>2.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create a Think Tank-style session to bring experts across the industry together to discuss creative solutions to specific concerns.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>3.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create a Wants/Needs Exchange board to connect people who wish to swap expertise or collaborate on a shared issue. Provide space for conversation after connections are made.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>4.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create opportunities for networking, but</span> <a href="https://www.speednetworking.com/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>match participants with AI</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>5.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Provide instruction on how to create a Mind Map and encourage attendees to capture their thoughts using this structured format.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Fun, play-based breakout sessions</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>6.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Host a</span> <a href="https://www.lego.com/en-us/seriousplay" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Lego® Serious Play® program</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">to encourage building and ideas-sharing through play.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>7.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Challenge attendees to let loose with Mario Kart battles; up the ante with a bracket-style competition for the duration of the event.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>8.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create a casino on-site—with a twist: All attendees compete to earn donations for charity.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>9.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Let the attendees show off their random (or business-based) knowledge with a Pub Trivia event. Randomly-drawn teams can encourage networking. There might be a person in each group who wrestles for control, but it may provide a lesson in team dynamics.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>10.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create your own version of a Family Feud®-style survey game. Include the full audience with a polling app for live answers, see how well teams can answer industry-specific questions, or poll the audience on a variety of subjects and have the teams guess their answers.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>11.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Design a Breakthrough Bingo game. Ask attendees to fill out a blank BINGO card with challenges they face, either personally or within the industry. After the cards are full, create small group pairings, either randomly or using common themes. Allow time for a 30-minute group brainstorm session. The goal is to find ways to approach the challenges listed. Participants mark off the challenges they’ve tackled on the card by listing two actionable ideas for each concern. The goal is to get a bingo. This activity builds connections, encourages problem-solving, increases collaboration, promotes active listening, and exercises critical thinking skills.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Easy and short breakout sessions</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>12.</b></span><a href="https://medium.com/@ckoster22/why-your-company-should-be-doing-lightning-talks-c84b32e8f82b" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Lightning talks</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">are short presentations in the vein of PechaKucha. Several short, concise presentations, usually under five minutes each, foster thought, conversation, and collaboration.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>13.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Encourage participants to use the “Yes, and…” technique: Suggestions from others should be met with acceptance (“Yes!), then followed by a communication-extending “…and” to improve communication and reduce judgment.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>14.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create space for a</span> <a href="https://www.mindtools.com/pages/article/newHTE_87.htm" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>goal-setting workshop</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">. Host a discussion, then encourage participants to create a list of top personal or professional goals for the next month, six months, year—and what success looks like. Defining specific objectives improves chances of success versus vague goals.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Teaching and learning event breakout sessions</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>15.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Combine training and competition like</span> <a href="https://www.saem.org/annual-meeting/meetings-and-events/events/simwars" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>SAEM’s SimWars</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">, a simulation-based experiential learning event.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>16.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">An Ask Me Anything (AMA) presentation is a popular Q&A format that encourages attendees to guide the session.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>17.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Host a Business Book Club discussion: Announce the book ahead of time so attendees have a chance to read and prepare. The facilitator can lead the discussion—or invite the author to act as the host. Include a book synopsis and relevant notes for attendees who didn’t read the book, but still want to participate.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>18.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">One-on-one mentoring sessions, scheduled in fifteen-minute or half-hour blocks with relevant experts. Pre-registration is a must for scheduling purposes.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>19.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Allow attendees to explore</span> <a href="https://hbr.org/2015/03/leadership-summits-that-work" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>leadership training</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">and styles and learn their personal approach.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Outdoors-based event breakout activities</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>20.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Send teams on a scavenger hunt outdoors or around the block. This popular activity also provides an opportunity to collaborate with businesses along the route as an incentive for future sponsorship.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>21.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Stage an outdoor relay race to get the blood flowing after the lunch break.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>22.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Build space within nature for attendees to disconnect from their tech. Use outdoor yoga, a guided walking tour, or team-building exercises to encourage participants to put down the phone and unwind.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Collaborative event workshop ideas</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>23.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Encourage group cooperation with a trip planning exercise. Create a challenge—visit every state park in Colorado or taste-test at the top 25 maple producers in Vermont—and task teams with mapping out the best route to meet the challenge. The catch? Except for finding</span> <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/about/mymaps/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>business locations on a custom map</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">, Google isn’t allowed for directions or times. Participants present their route and reasoning (plus challenges) to the full group.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>24.</b></span><a href="https://support.zoom.us/hc/en-us/articles/206476093-Getting-Started-with-Breakout-Rooms" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Invite virtual attendees</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">to join the in-person conversation, too.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>25.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Host a paper airplane making and flying competition, challenge teams to an egg drop contest, or facilitate a</span> <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H0_yKBitO8M" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>marshmallow challenge</u></a><span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">. Hand out awards—from “Longest Flight” to “Biggest Crash”—to the winners. The benefits of these activities include collaboration, problem-solving, and getting people up and moving. They’re fun and easy, but encourage networking and the sharing of ideas in a fresh way.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Opportunities for sharing</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>26.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Host an Open Mic event for The Moth-style storytelling or model an event after the TED Talks format.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>27.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Riff on the reality show with a Start-Up Shark Tank-style session: Invite makers, creators, inventors, and more to promote their ideas during a pitch session to earn prizes, investors, or mentoring.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>28.</b></span><a href="https://www.eventmanagerblog.com/organising-successful-roundtables" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Roundtable discussions</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">allow space to participate in discussion or debate in an interactive, equal-footing format.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>29.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Drop-In Discussions provide a low-pressure format for conversations, networking, and engagement.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>Learn something unrelated to the field</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>30.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Storytelling or writing workshops encourage creativity. Make sharing optional.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>31.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Create a beginners’ Learn to Code for hands-on learning—</span><a href="https://csfirst.withgoogle.com/s/en/home" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>Google’s kid-centric CS First program</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">is packaged and ready to go for group activities.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>32.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Spark culinary talents with a cooking class—or challenge confident chefs in friendly competition.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>33.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">An</span> <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gWahL0yjR5g" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank" style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><u>improv workshop</u></a> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">provides a chance to unwind, but also teaches interpersonal communication, boosts awareness, and improves focus.</span>

<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;"><b>34.</b></span> <span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Host a Paint and Sip party for the artistically inclined or curious. Try a Brew and Build if your audience prefers a more tactile activity.</span>

<span style="font-size:15.428571701049805pt;"><b>Create the best event breakout sessions!</b></span>
<span style="font-size:11.142857551574707pt;">Gauge interest, see where you succeeded, and learn what snags to watch for next time by sending post-event surveys to the participants. Their feedback is how you create a bigger, better breakout schedule at your next event.</span>
